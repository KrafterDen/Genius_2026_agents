# B03 real-data audit — 2026-09-25

## Access and provenance

The [OpenTextile-NIR Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/18269172), DOI `10.5281/zenodo.18269172`, exposes version 1.0 under **CC BY 4.0** and lists eight files. Six small files were retrieved through the record's own API after inspecting its license and manifest. Each byte count and MD5 matched the published file metadata; SHA-256 values, URLs and local paths are in `data/manifests/dataset_manifest.json`. The 10.8 GB SWIR cube and 37.5 MB RGB archive were unnecessary for a first item-level audit and were not transferred. Raw files and the manifest remain gitignored. The original metadata snapshot is `.agent/runtime/open_textile_zenodo_record.json` (SHA-256 `40a221b4206d552ba6df4e5675784dadc62795c534875208de0084fb6bc1be4d`). Attribution is to Sormunen, Mahlamäki, Satu-Marja Mäkelä and Mikko Mäkelä, with the dataset DOI and CC BY 4.0 license.

The [source article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12925456/) says pieces were cut from garments handled by a post-industrial waste operator; the operator received composition information from suppliers. These are **nominal supplier labels, not current-fibre chemical assays**. The paper warns that use and label errors can change the real composition. It also says 11 outlier flags came from repeated whole-dataset spectral-regression residual screening. Those flags may be diagnostic metadata but cannot be used as a global pre-split exclusion without leakage or selection bias.

## Physical units and measurements

The actual semicolon-delimited files contain **71 unique piece IDs** in each of the composition, SWIR mean spectrum, SWIR ROI-coordinate and RGB-mean tables. All ID sets agree. There are **70 conservative parent-prefix groups**: `sample_45-1-1` and `sample_45-2-1` share prefix `45`. This grouping is an inference, not confirmed garment provenance. Unknown additional dependence by supplier, garment lot or camera session cannot be ruled out. Each mean spectrum represents one selected rectangular region of a piece; a pixel or band is not another garment. The example reader was inspected but not run.

The small SWIR table has **288 wavelength columns from 953.04 to 2547.64 nm**, monotone, with one mean reflectance vector per piece. There are 20,448 nonempty finite entries; observed values span 0.018593097–0.8384839, without negative or over-one values. ROI bounds are nonempty for every piece. Per the article, cube data had dark/white-reference conversion to reflectance and dead-pixel removal before these means were released. The article's example PLS preprocessing is a later modeling choice and is not assumed to have been applied to this table. The original images and calibration references were not independently audited; no measurement can speak for a full multilayer garment.

Representative raw-vector averages over all bands are 0.552 for reported 100% cotton `sample_4-1`, 0.666 for reported 100% polyester `sample_15-1`, 0.608 for the 65/35 polyester/cotton `sample_2-1`, 0.630 for unknown `sample_24-1`, and 0.610 for outlier-flagged `sample_14-1`. The lowest and highest whole-vector means belong to `sample_34-1` and `sample_26-1`. These are descriptive acquisition checks, not a fitted model or evidence that a mean-brightness rule identifies fibres; colour, texture and geometry also affect reflectance.

## Composition audit and target capacity

The 71 rows include 57 unflagged nominal compositions, 11 outlier-flagged nominal compositions and 3 wholly unknown compositions. All 68 reported compositions sum to 100 percentage points; the 3 unknown rows contain no fibre shares. Empty fibre cells are **unlisted components conditional on a complete nominal row**, not independently established zero impurity. Reported entries appear as whole percentages with `.0`; underlying rounding tolerance and label accuracy are undocumented. Exact boundary values therefore remain nominal and potentially ambiguous. The machine-readable inventory and IDs are in `protocol/data_audit.json`; `protocol/target_feasibility.csv` records the scenario counts.

| Historical/context profile | Unflagged nominal eligible | Unflagged nominal ineligible | Flagged eligible / ineligible | Unknown | Relevant boundary evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| JRC 2024 ≥80% cotton mechanical proxy | 6 pieces / groups | 51 pieces / 50 groups | 1 / 10 | 3 | Two items report exactly 80%; two report 75%. |
| JRC 2024 ≥95% polyester chemical proxy | 10 pieces / groups | 47 pieces / 46 groups | 2 / 9 | 3 | No 94–95% reports; one 96%, two 97%, one 99%; most positives are 100%. |
| Infinited about 88% cotton preference | 4 pieces / groups | 53 pieces / 52 groups | 1 / 10 | 3 | The preference is approximate, not an exact acceptance rule. |

These are **composition-only, supplier-label scenarios**, not actual recycler acceptance decisions. Other process requirements, garment layers, contamination, trim and mass are unobserved. The 95% polyester proxy has more positives than the cotton proxy but still cannot support a strong selected-set risk statement. Even granting all 10 unflagged positives as independent accepted observations and observing zero false accepts, the one-sided 95% binomial upper risk bound would be about **25.9%**; after any train/calibration/evaluation split, fewer positive evaluation observations remain. That calculation is a capacity illustration, not a measured result. An uncalibrated item model, actual process threshold and valid exchangeability assumptions remain unverified.

## Independent alternative audit

[NIST NIR-SORT 2.0](https://data.nist.gov/od/id/mds2-3325) is a stronger candidate for later work because the [official description](https://www.nist.gov/mml/mmsd/security-technologies-group/near-infrared-spectra-origin-defined-and-real-world-textiles) enumerates 113 physical fabrics, including 90 known-provenance fabrics, and multiple instruments. The NIST RMM record API was retrieved as `.agent/runtime/nist_sort_record.json` (SHA-256 `34d04723486f3db81ec354af2a213823e1f8e50392cae0fbdbb09e3aea7ade6c`): it lists 170 components, a public access level, [NIST data terms](https://www.nist.gov/open/license), and nominal version 2.0.1. Its release dates have inconsistent ordering (v2.0.1 metadata update dated before v2.0.0 data release), so cite file hashes and actual content when obtained. Direct `data.nist.gov/od/ds/...` file requests for its small license and sample table stalled with zero bytes in bounded attempts. The [official data.gov catalog](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/near-infrared-spectra-of-origin-defined-and-real-world-textiles-nir-sort-a-spectroscopic-a) points to a [Materials Data Facility mirror](https://acdc.alcf.anl.gov/mdf/detail/7e12131d-87fe-435b-8de4-757bd52f78a5-1.0/), still being checked. **No NIST specimen-level composition counts or spectral file access are claimed.** The NIST descriptor distinguishes manufacturer/source A/B specifications from less verified care-label or product-card C/D labels.

[HyperTex](https://zenodo.org/records/21489250) files are embargoed through 2026-09-30. Its stated 198 captures cannot be counted as 198 independent chemically validated garments without its files and provenance, and it is not a current dependency.

## Decision and reproducibility

Use the retrieved OpenTextile small files as a **lawful, finite exploratory/reproduction base**. Narrow H02-1's risk/coverage claim to H02-2's grouped audit/reproduction question until a stronger labeled source is accessible and audited. No model was fit, no performance test or validation run, and no final evaluation information was used. Do not convert this inventory into a claimed selective-routing result. Reconsider the source choice when NIST small files or post-embargo HyperTex become accessible; keep instruments and label qualities separate.

From the repository root, reproduce the read-only inventory with `python scripts/audit_open_textile_metadata.py data/raw/open_textile_zenodo_18269172 > .agent/runtime/open_textile_audit.json`. Source files and metadata/hashes needed for the command are listed in `data/manifests/dataset_manifest.json`; the output of that command was saved during B03. No parser robustness fixture was created under the current no-tests instruction.
