# B04 risk and sample-size analysis

## Quantities that must remain separate

For a **fixed** automatic decision rule on an independently sampled population, composition-proxy selected-set risk is `P(nominally ineligible | ACCEPT)`. In a finite scored collection it is `FP/(TP+FP)`, with **accepted items** as the denominator. When `TP+FP=0`, precision/risk is undefined and automatic coverage is zero; review-all cannot be scored as a perfect sorter. The threshold's label is only the supplier nominal share, so even a low measured proxy risk would not establish risk against chemical composition or current recycler acceptance. `P(ACCEPT | nominally ineligible)` is a false-positive rate with a different denominator. Coverage, review rate, false decline, calibration error and mass contamination have still other denominators; exact definitions are in `protocol/metrics.json`.

Marginal prediction-interval coverage does **not** imply precision among selected items. A concrete counterexample: 95% of specimens truly have polyester share 90% and receive an interval [85%, 94%]; 5% also truly have 90% but receive [96%, 100%]. The intervals have 95% marginal coverage. If the rule accepts only intervals wholly above a 95% threshold, it accepts 5% of specimens and every accepted specimen is ineligible. Its selected-set false-acceptance risk is 100%. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a run on project data. [Jin and Candès](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v24/22-1176.html) explicitly study selection error separately from ordinary marginal conformal validity. Likewise, item-count precision cannot imply stream purity: item masses and true fibre fractions are absent.

## Capacity from the audited collection

For **zero observed failures among `n` genuinely independent accepted units** under a fixed rule and IID Bernoulli error model, the one-sided 95% exact upper bound on false-acceptance probability is `1 − 0.05^(1/n)`. This comes from solving `(1−p)^n = 0.05`; it is not a project outcome or an unconditional guarantee. Basic arithmetic gives:

| Hypothetical independent accepted, all error-free | One-sided upper bound |
|---:|---:|
| 6 (unflagged cotton positives at ≥80%) | 39.3% |
| 7 (all nominal cotton positives, including one flagged) | 34.8% |
| 10 (unflagged polyester positives at ≥95%) | 25.9% |
| 12 (all nominal polyester positives, including two flagged) | 22.1% |

At least `ceil(log(0.05)/log(0.95)) = 59` independent error-free accepted items would be needed for a bound below 5%; `ceil(log(0.05)/log(0.99)) = 299` for below 1%. The [NIST exact binomial reference](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbino.htm) documents the exact binomial construction. These counts are a deliberately optimistic planning upper capacity: the 6/10/7/12 are **nominal eligible labels**, not observed accepted items, and any disjoint training/calibration/evaluation allocation would leave fewer in evaluation. It is therefore unsupported to promise a useful ≤5% proxy false-acceptance certificate here. A loose bound or abstain-all policy might be mathematically valid under appropriate assumptions, but would not satisfy the project's practical objective.

Even this arithmetic would be overconfident as an empirical claim: 70 parent prefixes do not establish 70 independent supplier batches; the 11 flagged labels come from a model applied to the full collection; reported shares can differ from present-day fibre fractions; the collection is not a known random sample of future feedstock; and repeated folds share training observations. A five-fold output does not turn 67 labeled groups into `5 × 67` trials. [Bengio and Grandvalet](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/v5/grandvalet04a.html) explain why cross-validation variance estimation is delicate; resampling a small dependent collection should be reported as descriptive, not a formal generalization certificate.

## Methods considered and choice

- **Simple grouped nested internal evaluation:** chosen for the narrowed H02-2 reproduction. It keeps known dependent pieces together and tunes only within outer-training groups; [scikit-learn's grouped/nested guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html) supports the boundary. The pooled outer errors are exploratory because the source was already inspected, hidden dependence and label error remain, and no independent final collection exists.
- **Disjoint training/selection/calibration/final sets with exact binomial risk control or Learn-then-Test:** deferred. [Original LTT](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.01052v5) can control a prespecified population risk with high probability using valid calibration risk tests and multiplicity handling, under independent/exchangeable calibration, frozen models and rules, and matched deployment. The current source lacks credible positive capacity, assay labels and demonstrated sampling assumptions. Inner CV used for PLS complexity is **not** an LTT calibration set.
- **Conformal selection / cfBH:** [Jin and Candès](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v24/22-1176.html) provide an existing selection-specific method with expected false-discovery control under its own exchangeability/score assumptions. It is a serious future comparator, not an automatic guarantee for source-flagged textile labels. It controls a selection estimand distinct from a realized accepted batch's purity.
- **General conformal risk control or marginal intervals:** [Conformal Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814) targets its specified bounded monotone loss and assumptions. Substituting a marginal coverage number for conditional accepted-set risk is invalid; the counterexample above refutes the shortcut. Installing MAPIE or using a nominal confidence band is not a scientific contribution.

## Label and action sensitivity

The author-defined primary natural-share target includes all 68 nominally labeled pieces, including the 11 source flags, to avoid retrospectively dropping model-discordant observations. Report flagged and unflagged strata secondarily. The three unknown labels have no true supervised outcome and are not negatives. For a nominal 80% cotton report, the two exactly-80% pieces are boundary-ambiguous because the source's reporting precision and present-day composition are unverified. Polyester has no 94–95% nominal cases and most ≥95% positives are 100%; a threshold result cannot be generalized to near-boundary waste.

If a later illustrative rule accepts `a>0` pieces with `e` nominal-label errors, a hypothetical allowance that at most `k` **accepted labels** are wrong yields a simple sensitivity range `[max(0,e−k)/a, min(a,e+k)/a]`. The registered `k=1,2,3` values are assumptions, not an estimated supplier error model or chemical validation. For `a=0`, risk remains undefined. Wrong labels can be systematic, so no clean-label risk follows from a noisy-label proxy without additional assumptions; see the [original noisy-label conformal analysis](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1549/23-1549.pdf). A perfect reviewer is an oracle simulation against nominal labels only; actual reviewer accuracy, speed and capacity have not been measured.

**Conclusion for claims:** B04 preregisters an exploratory grouped comparison of nominal composition estimation. It cannot support selected-set precision, individual guarantees, current recycler acceptance, batch mass purity or avoided-emissions numbers. A later positive internal result may justify seeking independently assayed external data; a negative or inconclusive result is publishable as a dataset/protocol limitation.
